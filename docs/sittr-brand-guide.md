# Sittr — Brand Guide (Marketing Reference)

*Condensed from the full product specification, for quick reference in marketing work.*

## The one-line pitch
Sittr is a trusted pet-sitting marketplace for South Africa — built to replace Facebook groups by fixing what people actually complained about: no vetting, high hidden fees, and unreliable communication.

## Who we're talking to

**Owners:** People who currently ask around on Facebook for a pet sitter and feel uneasy about it — they want someone genuinely vetted, not just "a friend of a friend," without paying a fortune or a fee for a service they use twice a year.

**Sitters:** People already pet sitting informally (often already active on Facebook groups) who struggle to get consistent exposure and are tired of platforms taking 20–40% commission or charging signup fees just to be listed.

## What makes Sittr different (lead with these, not "we love pets")
1. **Real verification** — every sitter passes ID verification before going live. Criminal background checks (via HURU) are strongly encouraged and earn a visible "Background Checked" badge plus a Trust Score boost, but are currently optional rather than mandatory — be accurate about this distinction in any copy, don't imply every sitter is background-checked when they aren't.
2. **Low, transparent commission** — no membership fees on either side, full fee breakdown shown before every booking.
3. **No travel fees** — sitters only appear for bookings inside areas they've chosen to cover.
4. **Sitters set their own rates**, per service.
5. **SOS backup system** — if a sitter has a genuine emergency mid-sit, a vetted replacement steps in fast, with full access to everything already logged. No pet is ever left stranded.
6. **Boarding & daycare that actually takes bigger/less-common breeds** — sitters state openly which breeds they welcome, solving a real complaint (e.g. bulldog owners struggling to find boarding).

## Tone of voice
- Warm but not saccharine — steady and trustworthy, not "cute puppy" marketing
- Direct and transparent — the brand's whole pitch is honesty about fees and safety, so copy should never feel evasive or oversell
- Plainspoken South African warmth, not corporate-speak
- Confident about the trust/safety angle without fear-mongering about competitors or Facebook

## Visual identity

**Colors**
- Forest green `#2C4A3E` — primary
- Terracotta / clay `#C77A4E` — accent
- Warm off-white `#F7F5F1` — background
- Muted gold `#D9A441` — star ratings only

**Typography**
- Fraunces (serif) — headings, wordmark
- Manrope (sans) — body copy, UI text

## Real numbers worth knowing for content
- Commission is asymmetric by design: lower % from sitters, higher % from owners (sitter supply is the harder side to grow)
- 72-hour free cancellation window
- Sitters get paid once, released 2 days after a sit ends (not a slow multi-week wait)
- Criminal checks use HURU (500+ PostNet/Jetline locations, results often same-day) — same system Bolt uses for driver vetting in South Africa, not the slow multi-week SAPS route

## What NOT to say
- Don't imply verification makes the platform risk-free — it reduces risk, it doesn't eliminate it (this matters legally too, not just tonally)
- Don't disparage Facebook groups directly by name in outward-facing content — better to focus on what Sittr offers than to attack the alternative
- Don't overpromise instant sitter approval — verification takes at least some time, even if faster than people expect
- Don't say or imply "every sitter is background-checked" — that's currently false. Every sitter is ID-verified; criminal checks are optional and badge-marked. Owners should be able to filter for the badge specifically, and copy should make the distinction clear rather than blur it
